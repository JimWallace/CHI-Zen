# Transparency and open science

Rules for sharing materials, data and analysis code, preregistering, and anonymising for
review. Rule IDs are `TRN-n`. (The manual's "Transparency" section is still a stub that
points to OSF; this file is the current guidance.)

Why: assessing whether a contribution holds up requires access to the study materials, data
and code, and not sharing them limits others' ability to scrutinise or build on the work
[Wacharamanotham2020]. CHI strongly encourages supplementary material that supports
reproducibility, such as "survey text, experimental protocols, source code, and data"
[CHI2027].

**TRN-1. Share materials, or say why not.** Put study materials (questionnaires, interview
guides, stimuli, task instructions, consent forms), analysis code and, where ethics allow,
data in a public repository such as an OSF project (https://osf.io) or as supplementary
material, and cite it in the paper (REF-11). If something can't be shared, say what and why
(e.g. "transcripts are not shared to protect participants, as required by our REB").

**TRN-2. Add a short availability statement** (e.g. at the end of the method or in an
"Open Science" or "Data Availability" paragraph): what is shared, where, and under which
licence.

**TRN-3. Data from people stays within its consent.** Share only what consent and the REB
approval cover; for online health data that usually means no raw posts (see
[ethics.md](ethics.md), ETH-4 and ETH-6). Derived data (codes, counts, model outputs) can
often be shared when raw data can't.

**TRN-4. Preregister confirmatory studies** (hypotheses, design, sample size, analysis plan)
before collecting data, e.g. on OSF, and cite the preregistration. Preregistration lets
readers see that the evidence for a hypothesis is not the result of hypothesising after the
results are known (HARKing) [Cockburn2018; Nosek2018]. Report deviations from the plan, and
label unplanned analyses as exploratory (STAT-3).

**TRN-5. Anonymise everything for double-blind review.** CHI requires that "all video
figures and supplementary material must be anonymized throughout the review process", and
papers that violate the policy, including in supplementary material, can be rejected
[CHI2027]. CHI's anonymization policy [CHI2027-Anon] asks authors to:
- remove author and institution names from the title and header; changing their text colour
  is not enough. acmart's `anonymous` class option does this, and also leaves out `\thanks`,
  `\email`, `\orcid` and the `acks` environment [acmart];
- cite their own work in the third person ("As described by Chetty et al. [10]", not "As
  described in our previous work [10]"), without anonymising the reference; a reference
  marked "anonymous" is grounds for desk rejection (REF-12);
- leave out details that easily reveal who they are, such as where a study was run, where
  its approvals were obtained, or figures that show the authors' faces;
- keep grant details and partner organisations out of the acknowledgments;
- keep identifying information out of the document's metadata.

Wrap identifying text in acmart's `\anon{...}`, which prints "ANONYMIZED" in an anonymous
submission and the text itself once the option is removed [acmart]: `\anon{University of
Waterloo}`, `\anon{REB \#12345}`. Use OSF's view-only links with anonymisation for review,
remove names from file metadata (including the PDFs and images a figure includes, whose
metadata pdfTeX and LuaTeX copy into the paper's PDF), code comments and repository
histories, and replace self-identifying text (lab names, REB numbers, grant numbers) until
camera-ready. `TODO(Jim): source for OSF's anonymised view-only links, if we want to cite
OSF's help page.`

`check_paper.py` checks an anonymous submission for what it can find: author and institution
names in the body, first-person self-citation, ethics and grant numbers, acknowledgments in a
`\section`, a hand-set `pdfauthor`, an author in an included PDF's metadata, OSF links
without `view_only`, GitHub and GitLab links in a sentence about "our" code or data
(`W-anon-*`), and `.bib` entries by "Anonymous" (`E-anon-ref`).
Faces in figures, study sites described in other words, and supplementary material need a
person to check.

**TRN-6. Make the analysis reproducible from the shared files**: a README that says how to
run it, software versions (R/Python and package versions), and seeds for anything random.
Figures generated by scripts (see [figures.md](figures.md)) should be regenerable from the
shared data.
